For years, DEA agents pursued Le Roux across the globe as he eluded capture through advanced encryption and ruthless tactics. The story takes an unexpected turn when Le Roux is finally captured and strikes a deal to become a government informant, cooperating to bring down his own organization and associates. I'm normally not into true crime, but I do have a soft spot in my heart for investigative journalism. During the course of this book, I found myself disgusted with the so-called "good guys", kind of rooting for them in spite of that, and then just appreciating the work and research that Evan did in order to create a pretty comprehensive story of a very convoluted series of events.

With him starting off with an online pill-mill, I still find myself wishing the DEA took just 1% of the effort they spent on screwing over (or trying to) an octogenerian pharmacist and a young doctor who saw the potential of tele-medecine, and focused it on the [Sackler family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sackler_family){:target="_blank"}, a significantly more deadly criminal empire, but that's just not the world we live in.

It was a heck of a journey for sure, and does do a pretty good job of showing that so many of these rich people are really just gigantic losers, building empires and capital just to try (and fail) to buy the respect of their fellow humans, when it could be done for free by simply not being horrible people.
