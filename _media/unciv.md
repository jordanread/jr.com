Sid Meier's Civilization is one of those games that holds special memories for me. Sure, there were old LAN parties, but what I really remember about it was working graveyard shifts at a call center, before I even had my first career position. Turn based games were ideal, because while it was mostly dead, at least one of the 8 people on the team was usually on a call, and this made it so that the gameplay didn't have to stop, nor punish the person who was actually doing their job.

When I found Unciv, the README on the [github repo](https://github.com/yairm210/Unciv) actually had all the points right there that I was interested in.

> According to the [US Copyright Office FL-108](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/9/96/U.S._Copyright_Office_fl108.pdf), intellectual property rights do not apply to mechanics - as I'm sure you know, there are a billion Flappy Bird knockoffs.

The mechanics were what I was into. Sure the super polished UI and the absolutely *incredible* soundtrack (Christopher Tin's [Baba Yetu](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IJiHDmyhE1A) still gives me the chills) made the game a huge success, but I didn't *need* that to have a good time. Yet again, the README seemed to be just for me.

At the top, under the heading Is this any good?

> Depends what you're looking for. If you're in the market for high-res graphics, amazing soundtracks, animations etc, I highly recommend Firaxis's Civ-V-like game, "Civilization V".
>
> If you want a small, fast, moddable, FOSS, in-depth 4X that can still run on a potato, you've come to the right place :)

I don't spend a lot of time playing games, but Unciv is one I highly recommend. I even used setting up a server over the net as a project to motivate my nephew as he started digging into technology. Hasn't worked yet, but the computer is fun to play against as well.
