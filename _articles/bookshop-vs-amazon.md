It's the default because Amazon spent twenty-some years making sure it would be the default, and it worked.

But "default" and "good" aren't the same thing, and once I actually had to pick where those links go on purpose, instead of just going along with the reflex, Amazon stopped being the obvious answer.

Here's the short version: Amazon's whole model in books is to be the biggest bookstore in the world by being the worst possible neighbor to every other bookstore. It uses books as a loss-leading wedge to get you into a Prime subscription and an ecosystem, then leans on that scale to squeeze publishers on terms, squeeze authors on royalties, and squeeze warehouse workers on everything — quotas, injury rates, bathroom breaks, all of it, it's not exactly secret at this point. A sale on Amazon mostly enriches Amazon. The author gets their cut regardless of where you buy, but everyone *else* in that transaction — the people who make actual bookstores work — gets cut out.

Bookshop.org exists specifically to not do that. It's a genuine online storefront, but the money doesn't disappear into a single warehouse conglomerate — it's split so that a real chunk of each sale's profit margin goes to independent bookstores, including ones you'll never set foot in. You can pick a specific store to support, or just let it go into the general pool that gets split across all of them. It's a B Corp, which means the mission is actually load-bearing in how the company's structured, not a line in the "About" page. None of that makes it a *charity* — it's still a business, still taking a cut, still running affiliate programs like the one that funds this footnote. It just isn't optimized to be the last bookstore standing.

So that's the button you'll see on book entries here now: **Buy on Bookshop.org**, front and center. If I have an Amazon link for something too — sometimes I do, sometimes it's just what I had on hand from before I sorted this out — it's still there, but it's the small icon next to the real button, not the headline. I'm not going to pretend I've got some airtight ethical framework here. I still buy things on Amazon sometimes. But *this site's* defaults are mine to set, and given a totally free choice between "send a few cents toward warehouse quotas" and "send a few cents toward independent bookstores," I don't see a version of that decision where the second one isn't obviously better.

If you've got a favorite local bookstore already, Bookshop.org lets you buy from *them specifically* through the same site — that's a better option than either of my links. If you don't have one, I'd rather my little affiliate cut go toward keeping a bunch of them alive than toward whatever Amazon's doing with it.
